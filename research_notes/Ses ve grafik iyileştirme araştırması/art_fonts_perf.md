# Art Assets, AI Card Art, Fonts and Asset Performance (OnChainBattles)

Research date: 2026-11-04. Network note: kenney.nl, game-icons.net and craftpix.net could not be fetched from this session because they are outside the session's allowed network domains. Their terms below come from search-result snippets of their own pages or of third parties, and should be checked on the live pages before shipping. Google Fonts glyph tabs and caniuse.com were not fetched directly either.

Local baseline (measured in repo, `public/assets/`, 2026-11-04): about 18 MB of PNG in total (195 image files). `backgrounds/` holds 12 MB in 7 files. Each 1456x816 background is 1.6 to 2.4 MB (bg_menu.png and bg_main_menu.png are both 2,430,356 bytes, probably duplicates). `icons/` holds 2.4 MB in 15 files, `cards/` 1.5 MB in 74 files (art 140x90 plus thumbs), and `board/` 1.2 MB in 3 skins of about 714 to 720 px. In `src/`, 28 `fontFamily` uses are `"Courier New", monospace` and 4 are `Arial`. No webfont is loaded.

## (a) Free and commercial-use 2D asset sources: licenses, attribution, redistribution

### Takeaway
CC0 sources (Kenney) and CraftPix freebies need no attribution. game-icons.net needs per-author CC BY 3.0 credit. OpenGameArt mixes licenses and must be checked per asset, so prefer CC0, CC-BY or OGA-BY there and avoid GPL and CC-BY-SA art in a closed-source game. Every source forbids reselling the raw files. None of the sources checked settled whether NFT or on-chain use is allowed, which matters if crypto mode returns.

### Cited Findings
- game-icons.net: icons are provided "under the terms of the Creative Commons 3.0 BY license", so you can use them freely as long as you credit the original author. The suggested credit format is "Icons made by {author}. Available on https://game-icons.net". — [game-icons.net About](https://game-icons.net/about.html)
- game-icons.net icons are by individual authors (Lorc, Delapouite, Skoll, sbed, etc.). A common credit pattern is "Trophy icon, by Lorc (CC BY 3.0)" with a link to the icon page. Not every derived pack is CC BY, and repackagers sometimes add their own credit requests. — [OpenGameArt comment](https://opengameart.org/comment/23673); [shikashipx itch.io pack](https://shikashipx.itch.io/shikashis-fantasy-icons-pack/comments?before=14)
- Kenney assets are CC0 (public domain dedication). The live kenney.nl/support page was not fetched (blocked), so I have no direct quote. — Gap, see below
- CraftPix freebies may be used in personal and commercial projects. Attribution is not required but appreciated. The "Forbidden" section bans reselling source files or slightly modified versions, while selling games that contain the assets is allowed. — [CraftPix freebie license as summarized on free-game-assets.itch.io](https://free-game-assets.itch.io/free-pixel-art-dungeon-objects-asset-pack/comments); [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/3844037)
- OpenGameArt: when an upload lists several licenses, you may pick any one. The founder's view is that GPL and CC-BY-SA don't prohibit commercial use, but their share-alike terms "make certain business models impractical". The FAQ gives a sample credit: "Title" by Author licensed CC-BY 3.0. — [OpenGameArt FAQ](https://opengameart.org/content/faq)
- OpenGameArt contributors advise against GPL art in closed-source commercial games and say CC-BY-SA derivatives must stay CC-BY-SA. This is community opinion, not legal advice. — [OGA forum: licensing a game](https://opengameart.org/forumtopic/licensing-a-game)
- An NFT licensing explainer notes that buying an NFT does not transfer copyright. Minting third-party art as tokens would likely fall under "resell/redistribute" bans, but that is an inference, not stated by CraftPix. — [a16z "Can't Be Evil" licenses](https://goto.etherscan.com/rd/b31-license)

### Inferences
- Keep a `public/CREDITS.md` or in-game Credits screen listing each game-icons.net icon (name, author, CC BY 3.0, link). This is the only source in the set with a hard attribution duty.
- For UI chrome (frames, buttons, panels), Kenney CC0 packs carry the least legal risk, but their flat, clean style may clash with the existing painterly card art and will likely need a re-tint or overpaint.
- itch.io packs each carry their own license, often custom. Record the license text and purchase receipt per pack in the repo. Itch.io has no single license.
- Before enabling crypto stakes, re-read the CraftPix and itch.io license text for blockchain/NFT/token clauses. Gameplay stakes are not NFTs of the art, but some licenses ban any "crypto" use.
- Lospec palettes are colour lists and are generally not copyrightable as such. No source was checked for this.

### Gaps
- Kenney's exact license page text, game-icons.net's full author list, and CraftPix's full "2.2 Forbidden" section (including any NFT/crypto clause) were not readable because those domains are outside the session's allowed network list. Allow those domains (kenney.nl, game-icons.net, craftpix.net) and re-check.
- Lospec palette licensing was not researched.

## (b) AI-generated card art: tools, style consistency, ToS, copyright, Steam/EU disclosure, backlash

### Takeaway
The proven way to fill the remaining placeholder cards in the same style is a fixed prompt template plus style references: either a small style LoRA trained on the existing ~20 painterly cards (Scenario.gg hosted, or kohya/SDXL/Flux locally) or img2img/IP-Adapter from existing art. Purely prompted output is not copyrightable in the US (USCO Jan 2025), so the IP value lies in human selection, editing and paint-over. Steam (since Jan 2026) and the EU AI Act (Art. 50, from 2 Aug 2026) both push toward disclosing player-facing AI art. Card-game communities punish undisclosed AI art hardest (Wizards of the Coast, Jan 2024).

### Cited Findings
- US Copyright Office, *Copyright and AI Part 2: Copyrightability*, released 29 Jan 2025: "given current generally available technology, prompts alone do not provide sufficient human control to make users of an AI system the authors of the output." Using AI as an assistive tool does not remove protection for human contributions such as selection, arrangement and modifications. No legislative change is needed. — [Copyright Office NewsNet 1060](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html?loclr=licop); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/); [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/02/07/u-s-copyright-office-issues-report-on-artificial-intelligence-and-copyrightability/)
- Most jurisdictions, including the EU, Japan, South Korea and China, require human authorship. Part 3 of the report covers training on copyrighted works. — [Akin AI tracker](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/us-copyright-office-releases-part-2-of-ai-report)
- Steam updated its AI disclosure form around 16–17 Jan 2026. Developers must disclose AI-generated content players consume (in-game art, audio, narrative, marketing/store assets) and content generated live during play. Behind-the-scenes "efficiency tools" such as code assistants no longer need disclosure. Valve also added an overlay button for reporting illegal AI content. This comes from press coverage; I found no Valve primary text in results. — [PC Gamer via reader](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-updates-AI-disclosure-form-requiring-developers-to-report-visible-and-in-game-AI-but-not-background-tools.1206103.0.html); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines)
- EU AI Act Art. 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 Aug 2026. Providers must machine-mark synthetic output (e.g. watermark, C2PA). Deployers (publishers) must label deepfakes. For evidently artistic, creative or fictional works, disclosure may be done so it "does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work", and a credits or description line may be enough. Content circulating before 2 Aug 2026 reportedly needs no retroactive label. Penalty figures conflict between sources (3% vs 1.5% of turnover). — [Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/technology/ai-transparency-ai-act-what-businesses-need-know-before-2-august-2026/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nfqg/always-read-the-label-eu-ai-transparency-rules-and-questions-that-remain-unanswe/); [Mondaq: draft Code of Practice](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/new-technology/1737838/european-commission-publishes-draft-code-of-practice-on-ai-labelling-and-transparency); [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/world-news/eu-ai-act-what-changes-as-new-transparency-rules-take-effect-from-august-2-126073000211_1.html)
- Midjourney ToS: users generally own their assets, but a company (or an employee of one) with more than US$1M in gross revenue in the prior year must be on the Pro or Mega plan to own them. The terms also grant Midjourney a perpetual licence to content, and on most plans images are public by default unless you use Stealth mode. These terms come from third-party summaries; the official text was not retrieved. — [Midjourney ToS (official)](https://docs.midjourney.com/docs/terms-of-service); [terms.law 2026 guide](https://terms.law/2026/01/15/midjourney-commercial-use-rights-complete-2026-guide/); [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/midjourney/midjourney-terms-of-service/revenue-based-asset-ownership-threshold/)
- Adobe Firefly: Adobe claims no IP rights in outputs and markets them as commercially safe, trained on licensed or Adobe Stock content. Contractual IP indemnification is only for eligible enterprise offers, not free or individual plans. Partner (non-Adobe) models inside Firefly are excluded from the indemnity. — [Adobe Firefly enterprise legal FAQ 2024-06](https://adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/pdf/enterprise/firefly-legal-faqs-enterprise-customers-2024-06-11.pdf); [beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/best-ai-image-generator-indemnity-firefly-vertex-imagen-openai-getty-shutterstock)
- OpenAI extends output indemnity ("Copyright Shield") to API and ChatGPT Enterprise customers. OpenAI's image-output ownership clause was not verified in this pass. — [beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/best-ai-image-generator-indemnity-firefly-vertex-imagen-openai-getty-shutterstock)
- Scenario (scenario.gg/scenario.com) workflow: pick 10+ representative images of your style, caption them accurately, train a style model or LoRA on a chosen base model, then generate with it. Scenario documents consistent-character and production workflows. Reported pricing is a limited free tier, paid from about $15/mo and Studio about $200/mo (secondary source). — [Scenario: training a style LoRA](https://help.scenario.com/en/articles/training-a-style-lora); [Scenario: inventory style](https://scenario.com/post/training-custom-style-for-inventory-items); [datastudios](https://www.datastudios.org/post/scenario-custom-trained-ai-game-asset-generation-style-consistency-and-pricing-explained)
- Practitioner advice for card sets: keep a fixed base prompt and vary only the subject, and train a small style LoRA on 10–15 reference images (about an hour in kohya, self-hosted). A more general pipeline is: style reference (existing art or LoRA), then ControlNet for composition, then IP-Adapter for consistency. These are community posts. — [Reddit snapshot 2026-04](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1smb4k4/snapshots/2026-04-17T17%3A15%3A22.941568Z); [skillselion AI game art](https://skillselion.com/skills/omer-metin/skills-for-antigravity/ai-game-art-generation)
- Wizards of the Coast, Jan 2024: WotC first denied that a Magic promo image used AI, then admitted a vendor had used AI tools ("we made a mistake"), and said it would review its vendor and AI policies. Earlier incidents included AI-"polished" art in D&D's *Glory of the Giants* (Aug 2023). Magic artist Dave Rapoza left over the affair. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/wizards-of-the-coast-reverses-course-admits-to-using-ai-in-promotional-image-well-we-made-a-mistake-earlier); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/wizards-of-the-coast-will-adjust-generative-ai-policy-for-magic-following-controversy/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/wizards-of-the-coast-admits-that-we-made-a-mistake-after-ai-art-confusion)

### Inferences
- Lowest-friction path for the remaining placeholder cards: use the ~20+ existing painterly 140x90 cards (ideally their larger source files) as a style reference set, generate at a much larger size (e.g. 1024x660 or more), paint over and fix by hand, then downscale. A human paint-over both improves consistency and strengthens the copyright claim (USCO "modifications" route).
- Train the LoRA only on art you own. If the existing cards were AI-generated themselves, their copyright status is already weak, so check their provenance.
- Disclosure practice: a one-line in-game Credits/About note ("Some card illustrations were created with AI-assisted tools and edited by hand"), plus a Steam disclosure if the game ever ships there. Never deny AI use when asked. WotC's damage came mainly from the denial, then the reversal.
- Pick tools by plan terms. Midjourney is fine below $1M revenue on Basic/Standard but images are public unless Stealth is used. Firefly is the most "commercially safe" positioning. Local SDXL/Flux keeps everything private (check the specific model licence; Flux variants differ). This is unverified here.
- In crypto mode, collectors and artists are especially sensitive to AI art. Pair any AI art with clear disclosure.

### Gaps
- Exact current Midjourney, OpenAI (gpt-image) and Leonardo ToS clauses were not fetched from the primary pages.
- Flux model licences (dev vs schnell vs pro) were not verified.
- No primary Valve/Steamworks text was found, only press.
- I found no specific 2025–2026 card-game backlash case beyond WotC 2023–2024 in results.
- No EU court decision on AI output copyright was found.

## (c) Fonts: medieval/fantasy display + readable body, Turkish support, Phaser loading, OFL

### Takeaway
Use one OFL display font for titles and card names (Cinzel is the strongest candidate, though it is all-caps) and one highly legible OFL body font with full latin-ext coverage (Inter, Source Sans 3 or Noto Sans). Self-host them, and load them before any Phaser Text is created, via Phaser's `this.load.font` (FontFace API) or `document.fonts.load`. OFL lets you embed fonts in a commercial game. You just can't sell the font alone, and a modified or subsetted copy can't keep a Reserved Font Name.

### Cited Findings
- Font Squirrel lists Cinzel (Natanael Gama) among Turkish-supporting fonts. Cinzel is all-caps in Trajan style: lowercase letters are small capitals, so ğ, ş and ı render as small-cap forms. — [Font Squirrel Turkish list](https://www.fontsquirrel.com/fonts/list/language/turkish/100); [Cinzel README (CTAN)](https://mirror.las.iastate.edu/tex-archive/fonts/cinzel/README); [Google Fonts Cinzel](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Cinzel/about?query=cinz)
- Phaser 4 has a `FontFile` loader type that "loads web fonts using the browser's FontFace API". Once loaded, the font is added to `document.fonts` and can be referenced by key in a Text `fontFamily`. On failure it logs a warning and continues. — [Phaser 4 API: Loader.FileTypes.FontFile](https://docs.phaser.io/api-documentation/4.0.0/class/loader-filetypes-fontfile)
- Phaser's v4.1.0 example uses WebFont Loader to make sure fonts are ready before Text renders. — [Phaser example: custom webfont](https://phaser.io/oss/examples/v4.1.0/game%20objects/text/view/custom-webfont)
- Known pitfall: a Text object created before the font has downloaded renders with a fallback font and won't re-render by itself. Force the download (e.g. `await document.fonts.load('16px "Cinzel"')`) during preload. — [Mozilla Hacks: webfont preloading for HTML5 games](https://hacks.mozilla.org/2016/06/webfont-preloading-for-html5-games/) (2016, older but the mechanism still applies); [codegenes guide](https://www.codegenes.net/blog/how-to-use-a-local-font-in-phaser-3/)
- OFL FAQ: embedding an OFL font "does not change the license of the document". Web pages using the fonts are fine (CSS is recommended). You can't sell the font by itself. A modified version can't use a Reserved Font Name, and "this includes subsetting for web fonts", unless the author gives written permission. — [OFL FAQ](https://ctan.csail.mit.edu/fonts/greek/gfs/gfsbaskerville/doc/OFL-FAQ.txt); [Wikipedia: SIL OFL](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIL_OFL)

### Inferences
- Suggested pairing:
  - Cinzel (or Cinzel Decorative for the logo only) for headings, card names and big numbers.
  - Inter, Source Sans 3 or Noto Sans for card rules text, HUD and buttons at 12–16 px.
  - Replace Courier New entirely. Monospace hurts readability and tone, though a tabular-figures body font such as Inter keeps aligned numbers.
  - Blackletter faces (Pirata One, Grenze Gotisch, Uncial Antiqua, MedievalSharp) are logo or flavour only. They are illegible at small sizes, and their Turkish coverage must be checked per font in the Google Fonts "Glyphs/Language" tab.
- Turkish check: verify the latin-ext subset contains ğ Ğ ş Ş ı İ ç ö ü for each chosen font. Inter, Source Sans 3, Noto Sans and EB Garamond are broad-coverage families, but this was not verified in this pass. Display fonts with limited coverage will drop to fallback glyphs mid-word.
- Self-host the WOFF2 files (e.g. via `@fontsource/*` npm packages or downloads in `public/fonts/`) instead of the Google Fonts CDN. This avoids privacy and GDPR issues, keeps the files offline-cacheable, and lets Vite fingerprint them. Keep the OFL.txt next to them. If you subset, the Google Fonts files usually have no Reserved Font Name, but check each OFL.txt.
- For text that must render the same crisp way at small sizes and scales (damage numbers), a BitmapFont generated from the chosen OFL font (e.g. with SnowB BMF or msdf-bmfont) is an option. Redistributing a rendered bitmap of an OFL font is generally treated as fine under the OFL FAQ, but confirm this.

### Gaps
- Per-font Turkish glyph verification on Google Fonts pages (Cinzel, Almendra, IM Fell, Pirata One, Grenze Gotisch, Uncial Antiqua, MedievalSharp, EB Garamond, Inter, Source Sans 3) was not done because the pages were not fetched. This must be checked manually or by inspecting the font files (e.g. `fc-query` or fonttools).
- No quantitative readability study for game UI at 12–16 px was found.

## (d) Asset performance: atlases, formats, compression, resolutions, budgets

### Takeaway
The biggest win is the backgrounds. Five 1456x816 PNGs at 1.6–2.4 MB each (12 MB) could become WebP/AVIF at roughly 100–300 KB each (estimate, not measured). WebP is about 96% and AVIF about 93% supported globally. Pack the card art, thumbnails and icons into one or two atlases. Phaser's compressed-texture loader supports KTX(v1)/PVR with an image fallback but has no confirmed KTX2/Basis support, so it isn't worth the complexity at this scale.

### Cited Findings
- Browser support: Can I Use (June 2026 data, via a blog) lists WebP at about 95.97% and AVIF at about 93.42% global support. AVIF shipped in Chrome 85, Firefox 93 and Safari 16.4, and WebP in Safari 14. Figures vary by blog by a few points. — [DEV: Is AVIF ready 2026](https://dev.to/swapfileio/is-avif-ready-for-production-in-2026-browser-support-and-real-trade-offs-4ani); [collectivebrain.de](https://collectivebrain.de/avif-statt-jpg-webp-2026/); [speedvitals](https://speedvitals.com/blog/webp-vs-avif/)
- Phaser compressed textures: the `CompressedTextureFile` loader (since 3.60, present in Phaser 4 docs) takes PVR or KTX containers. It supports a single texture, texture plus JSON atlas, or TexturePacker multi-atlas. It accepts an IMG fallback for unsupported GPUs, is WebGL-only, and needs URLs that include the file extension. — [Phaser 4 API: CompressedTextureFile](https://docs.phaser.io/api-documentation/4.0.0/class/loader-filetypes-compressedtexturefile); [Phaser 3.60 CompressedTextureFileEntry](https://newdocs.phaser.io/docs/3.60.0/Phaser.Types.Loader.FileTypes.CompressedTextureFileEntry)
- The Phaser v4.1.0 texture parsers list KTX (v1, formats ETC/ASTC/S3TC/PVRTC) and PVR, with no KTX2/Basis. KTX2/Basis exists only in third-party loaders (e.g. @stowkit/phaser-loader, texforge). — [Phaser textures.parsers](https://phaser.io/oss/docs/api-documentation/namespace/textures-parsers.md); [@stowkit/phaser-loader](https://app.unpkg.com/@stowkit/phaser-loader@0.1.16)
- free-tex-packer-core (npm) options include max size, power-of-two sizing, padding, rotation, trim, export formats (Phaser/Pixi JSON) and an optional TinyPNG `tinify` step. Phaser has featured Free Texture Packer as a free TexturePacker alternative. FastPack (Rust) offers oxipng lossless compression and a Phaser 3 export. — [free-tex-packer-core](https://github.com/odrick/free-tex-packer-core); [Phaser news: Free Texture Packer](https://phaser.io/news/2020/02/free-texture-packer); [FastPack](https://www.mintlify.com/Hexeption/FastPack)
- Repo measurement (this session): backgrounds total 12 MB of the 18 MB PNG total. Card art is 140x90 and the board skins are about 714–720 px square.

### Inferences
- Concrete actions, ranked by payoff:
  1. Convert backgrounds to WebP (quality about 80) or AVIF and drop duplicates (bg_menu = bg_main_menu by byte size). Phaser loads WebP and AVIF through the normal `this.load.image`, since it relies on the browser's image decoding (inference: not explicitly confirmed in docs fetched). Keep a PNG fallback only if old Safari matters.
  2. Run `pngquant --quality=70-90` then `oxipng -o 4 --strip safe` on the remaining PNGs (icons 2.4 MB in 15 files looks oversized for icons). Typical lossy-palette savings are large but were not measured here.
  3. Pack the 31 card arts, 31 thumbs, frames and icons into 1–2 atlases of 2048x2048 or less with free-tex-packer, exported as a Phaser JSON hash. This cuts HTTP requests and texture binds.
- High-DPI: card art drawn at 140x90 CSS px needs 280x180 source pixels for sharp 2x display, and 100x100 thumbs need 200x200. The 31 cards at 280x180 plus 200x200 fit in one 2048 atlas (31 x 280x180 is about 1.56 Mpx and 31 x 200x200 about 1.24 Mpx, under the 4.2 Mpx of a 2048² atlas). Generate new AI or commissioned art at 4x or more (e.g. 560x360+) and downscale.
- Lazy loading: load hub and menu assets in PreloadScene, then load battle-only assets (board skins, card atlas) in a later scene preload or in the background after the Hub appears.
- Budget: no authoritative "casual web game budget" figure was found. A first load (before entering the hub) of a few MB, well under the current 18 MB, is a reasonable internal target; the figure is an inference.
- Vite: files in `public/` are copied as-is with no hashing or optimization. Moving assets to `src/` imports, or adding `vite-plugin-image-optimizer`, would give hashing and compression. This comes from general Vite knowledge and was not re-verified here.
- KTX2/Basis is not recommended now. It has no official Phaser support, needs a WASM transcoder (a few hundred KB) and only pays off for large VRAM-heavy games.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for a recommended total download budget for casual or web portal games (CrazyGames/Poki publish SDK size guidance, but it was not retrieved).
- Phaser 4 RC6 handling of AVIF/WebP was not explicitly confirmed in docs.
- Actual compression ratios for this repo's PNGs were not measured. Running `cwebp` or `avifenc` on one background would settle it in seconds.
- Vite asset handling was not re-verified against vite.dev docs.
